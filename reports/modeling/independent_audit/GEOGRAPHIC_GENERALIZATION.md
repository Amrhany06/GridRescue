# Geographic transfer

**VERIFIED.** Unseen New York loses prevalence, discrimination and operating recall. The comparison below uses a single frozen model and cutoff. The common-prior AP reweights positives by 0.2032/group prevalence and negatives by 0.7968/(1−group prevalence), preserving each group's score distributions within class. This is a descriptive standardization. It does not assume population shift in reality is confined to the class prior.

The reduction depends on the reference distribution and cannot be interpreted as a causal fraction. With NY prevalence and the seen TPR/FPR, Bayes' rule gives about 14.85% PPV; actual NY PPV is 14.29%. At the same threshold NY has *both* lower TPR and lower FPR (21.31% versus 29.52%). The base rate explains much of this particular PPV comparison. The common-prior ranking gap, ROC AUC and matched-recall results demonstrate a second problem.

**STRONGLY SUPPORTED — risk scores transfer unevenly.** In NY the 0.3–0.4 bin averages score 0.343 and realized risk 0.147, versus 0.346 and 0.323 in seen counties. NY's aggregate mean score is 2.46× its prevalence even as the global rule misses 59.64% of NY positives. In California, 63.98% of rows flag, with 45,467 FP (35.06% of all FP) and 79.75% recall. In Pennsylvania only 4.68% flag, with 13,023 FN (25.27% of all misses) and 9.27% recall, although three PA counties were seen. Lowering the NY threshold to recover recall increases its low-prevalence workload. Different error profiles in represented states rule out unknown-state encoding as a complete explanation.

**PLAUSIBLE — unseen-state encoding and county-specific reporting schedules contribute.** The actual fitted encoder has no NY category. Its zero block is an unseen combination for a one-hot state block. It cannot learn a NY-specific offset or state × hour interactions during training. Two NY counties peak eight hours later in observed labels than in mean score; omitting hour changes state-wide AP by only +0.00034. Unequal service territories, outage severity, local time, source coverage and utility reporting could also explain these patterns. One four-county state holdout cannot identify a causal effect of state encoding or a typical transfer penalty. Archived county-level counts and ranks are in `reports/modeling/precision_diagnostic/results/development_metrics.csv`.
