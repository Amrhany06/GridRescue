# GridRescue independent scientific audit and remediation plan

**Decision: retain the experimental XGBoost benchmark. Fix the evaluation design before spending on 3M–8M training.** The local pipeline validly predicts a specific retrospective outcome: among positive county outage reports followed by 12 observed quarter-hours, will any reported count exceed or equal the current count plus the larger of 200 customers and 50%? There is no P0 finding invalidating the historical row scores. There is insufficient evidence to interpret those scores as physical incident detection, incident-level precision, or nationwide prospective performance.

## Verified benchmark

The frozen CPU XGBoost uses state, UTC hour and extended history, 1,053,328 training rows from 12 counties in 2018–2020, and 582,532 evaluation rows from 20 counties in 2021. Of 118,355 positives it flagged 66,810 and missed 51,545. Of 464,177 negatives it flagged 129,696 and cleared 334,481. Recomputed AP **0.411168**, recall **56.45%**, precision **34.00%**, accuracy **68.89%**, and **222.6 false-positive rows per 1,000 eligible rows**. The exact selected cutoff was 0.2670107781887054. It was selected on 890,035 chronological out-of-fold predictions and applied with strict greater-than. The later diagnostic's 60% recall crossing is a slightly different cutoff with the same 2021 decisions.

The project has revisited 2021 repeatedly. Its figures are trustworthy descriptions of this cohort, with limited value as independent confirmation of a newly chosen intervention. The official earlier model, its 0.5 protocol and the later experimental champion have separate lineages. No replacement of either is indicated.

## Ranked explanations and what would change the conclusion

The ranks prioritize evidence and impact on this decision. They are overlapping explanations; the percentages cannot be added as attributable fractions.

| Rank and hypothesis | Supporting evidence and importance | Counterevidence or limit | Classification and decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Moderate score separation, the class base rate and a recall-oriented global cutoff produce a high row burden. | 20.32% prevalence, 33.73% flagged. In 87,765 rows immediately above the cutoff through 0.35, only 25.31% crossed. At 0.5 precision reaches 61.73%, but recall falls to 13.07%. | AP is twice the constant-score baseline. An operating rule alone cannot lift precision at a fixed recall. | **VERIFIED** from scores, confusion counts and frozen-rule replay. E1/E4 distinguish improved ranking, risk calibration and alert burden. |
| 2. Geographic mix changes the base rate, ranking and transfer of an absolute score. | Seen vs NY prevalence 23.88% vs 8.09%, AP 0.4526 vs 0.1330. At a common 20.32% prior AP is still 0.4072 vs 0.3050. PA contributes 13,023 misses and CA 45,467 FP. | NY has lower FPR than seen counties. Heterogeneity also exists among seen counties. The responsible utility, state encoding and physical regime cannot be isolated from one held-out state. | **VERIFIED** shift and residual ranking gap; unknown-state encoding as a cause **PLAUSIBLE**. Run E2 before claiming a geographic fix. |
| 3. Small initial counts and little recent change identify escalations the model frequently misses. | 24,035 misses begin at ≤50 customers. After standardizing county and UTC hour, miss rates among positives were 57.9% at ≤50 and 38.4% above 50. At matched county/hour/severity, zero 60-minute change had 63.3% misses versus 43.5% for nonzero change. | The target's absolute floor makes these large proportional jumps. Many misses still have changing counts; observed flatness cannot establish that the onset was physically sudden. | **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** association on common strata. E3 tests whether an actionable precursor exists. |
| 4. UTC hour captures a strong, imperfectly transferable regularity in recorded escalations. | Removing hour costs 0.0744 AP; paired week-block 95% range 0.0671–0.0813. A training-only state × hour lookup earns 0.3470 AP. Kings and Queens have an eight-hour score/label peak mismatch. | The no-hour model scarcely changes NY AP. Hour can encode physical diurnal variation and reporting cadence even with complete slots. | **VERIFIED** predictive reliance; attribution to reporting cadence **INCONCLUSIVE**. Keep hour as control; isolate clock, cadence and state effects in E2/E3. |
| 5. The recorded peak target mixes persistent rises with transient crossings, under retrospective reporting selection. | 24.25% of positives cross in only one slot. 60,359 otherwise clean 2021 anchors lack complete futures; 5,277 already contain an observed crossing. Most FP outcomes are far below the boundary. | No physical reference, arrival timestamp or intervention study. Neither label error nor an irreducible performance ceiling can be quantified from the archive. | **VERIFIED** measurement/selection limitation; a limit on attainable AP from missing external drivers is **PLAUSIBLE**. E1/E5 establish sensitivity; E6 measures incremental forecast information. |

Recent missing history as the *dominant* source of misses is **NOT SUPPORTED**. It matters locally: on common county/hour/severity strata, incomplete vs complete past coverage has 44.0% vs 32.3% misses, but full-history rows account for 47,390 of 51,545 misses overall. An excess of immediate first-slot crossings among missed positives is also **NOT SUPPORTED** (10.09% of FN, 10.98% of TP). For standardization, retain positive-only strata with ≥5 observations per comparison arm and weight both arms by the stratum's total positive count. The three comparisons above retain respectively 118,314, 54,507 and 49,192 positives. These describe common-support subsets and do not adjust for all confounding.

## Work order for Sol High

| Priority | First implementation and ownership | Deliverable and decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| P0 · correctness | Preserve the reconstructed anchor/label and feature comparisons as regression gates before changing implementation. | Zero mismatched keys, labels, null masks or boundary-crossing horizons. A failure blocks downstream comparisons and triggers investigation of affected artifacts. Current audit passed. |
| P1 · denominator, evaluation and provenance | **First**, build E1 beside `reports/modeling/precision_diagnostic/diagnostic.py` using saved predictions. Make the denominator, eligibility, recall floor, alert unit and comparator explicit. Bind resumed local fold outputs in `local_improvement/train_local.py::tune_one` to code, settings and input content. | Reproduce the table above and coverage counts before any new fit. Flip one label, shift one timestamp and mutate one cached predictor while preserving its key; each wrong comparison must fail. An unbound local resume is a verified implementation hazard, with no demonstrated effect on current scores. |
| P1 · geography and scale design | Freeze E2 outer states and folds, first-report-only alert matching and comparisons. Amend `reports/modeling/cloud_expansion/experiment_manifest.json` and bundle only after approval. | Publish blind split memberships, hash identities, threshold provenance and common OOF policy population. Explicitly choose a combined breadth-and-volume experiment or include same-volume/same-geography controls. |
| P2 · precision and transfer | Run the small local E2/E3 ablations first: no state vs state, cadence and local-time variants, original vs extended history on the exact same folds. Replay E4 calibration/alert policies on earlier OOF and later scored blocks. | Apply predeclared paired AP, matched-recall PPV, actual recall, FP burden and state gates. Recalibration must improve held-out probability quality. Grouping must improve timely workload metrics. Do not count a cut in recall as a ranking gain. |
| P2 · cloud and target sensitivity | Run Tier A only after design freeze. Gate B/C on fixture, identities, parity and resources. Evaluate E5 as a separately named target with new labels/fits. | [Cloud gates](COLAB_READINESS_REVIEW.md) and [intervention acceptance tests](PRECISION_RECALL_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md). Retain and report negative results. |
| P3 · paper extensions | Seek independent incident timestamps and authorized as-of weather forecasts, prospectively selected states, further time windows and stronger matched baselines. | Require a measurable incremental effect or a bounded negative finding. Report uncertainty across times and states and physical-event linkage coverage. Preserve the sealed final dataset for a separately authorized final evaluation. |

**Colab verdict: READY WITH CONDITIONS.** The packaged code can test combined expansion against the frozen pilot. Runtime parity is untested on Linux. Its nested county tiers cannot identify a pure sample-size effect and its single unseen state cannot establish national transfer. Freeze the scientific comparison first, verify Tier A next, and only then authorize a larger fit. Existing local results need no wholesale rerun. New model, state, target and weather comparisons require new controlled fits. Calibration and alert-policy diagnostics can start with archived scores.

The five project skills informed distinct checks. **Feature engineering** drove the independent exact-offset, missingness and fold-preprocessing reconstruction. **Imbalanced data** drove the denominator, recall-floor and fixed-threshold replays. **Model evaluation** drove matched anchors, common-prior AP, calibration and paired calendar blocks. **Reproducible ML** drove source, model and notebook byte checks and saved-model scoring. **ML debugging** drove layer-by-layer isolation and tests of competing explanations using FP/FN margins, timing and complete-history subsets. The guide and pinned manifest were read locally. Seven diagnostic unit tests passed. All new calculations were development-only; large model fits, a fresh Spark run, online provider revalidation, Colab execution and final-holdout evaluation were outside this audit.
